certus_re

Reverse engineering of multilayer optical coatings, with the degrees of freedom on the table.

Open-source implementation, measured spectra and nominal designs accompanying “Transferability of Single-Layer Optical Constants to Complex Multilayer Reverse Engineering in the MWIR”, F. Lemarchand and J. Lumeau (2026).

This is the second deposit of a two-part study. The first, Certus Index Spline, determined the complex refractive index of two sputtered oxides on single layers. This one examines how that determination transfers to multilayer coatings produced on the same machine, and documents the assumptions and sensitivity tests used in the comparison.

This page is a guide. README.md is the reference, and python reproduce.py is the only thing that settles an argument: it re-derives every number quoted here, in a few minutes, from two dependencies and no compiler.

Why it exists

Multilayer reverse engineering conventionally adjusts layer thicknesses and dispersion corrections inside the same optimization. When the residual falls, the improvement cannot be attributed to the thickness correction — the physically meaningful outcome — rather than to the index correction, which silently rewrites the quantity one claims to validate.

A reverse-engineering procedure that is free to reshape n(λ) cannot, by construction, establish that a previously determined n(λ) was correct.

So this package does not let a run move anything it has not declared. The parameter blocks a run may release are written in the study file, counted before the run starts by the same code that assembles the parameter vector, and printed next to every residual. A held block still appears, with the reason it is zero: 0 (imposed) and 0 (not used) are different statements, and the difference is the whole argument.

What it handles

Three production optical filters on silicon (Filter 1 AR6, Filter 2 BS45, Filter 3 BS17) alongside two reference single layers on sapphire (SiO₂ and Nb₂O₅), inverted individually or jointly (41 parameters across 2266 data points). Two-side-coated components are also supported and tested.

Also: any angle of incidence, in s, p or the unpolarized channel; reflectance, transmittance and transmittance relative to the bare substrate; absorbing layers and substrates; a finite beam cone modelled as a piecewise-constant function of wavelength; polarizers treated as ideal (crosstalk excluded from investigation); and the local covariance uncertainty on every retrieved layer thickness.

The studies are a ladder

Each rung adds exactly one thing to the one before it, so that every change in a residual can be attributed to one cause.

Rung 0 is not a fit. It releases nothing, so its residual cannot have been bought, and every other rung is read against it.

studies/volet2/counter_experiments/ holds seven studies that are wrong by construction — the design written in the wrong order, a ground rear face modelled as polished, a dispersionless substrate table, the angle shifted by a quarter of a degree, the spectral mesh decimated, the search window opened, an index correction released. They exist so that the cost of each mistake is a number a reader can reproduce rather than a warning in a paper. Each costs more than the effect the article sets out to measure.

Four things to know before quoting a number

The problem is ill-posed, and the deposit regularizes it physically

Many thickness vectors reproduce one spectrum to within the photometry. Omitting the technological process prior (σproc → ∞) changes the residual of the joint inversion by less than 0.03 percentage points — well below the measured photometric repeatability — while adjacent layers undergo severe unphysical compensations, drifting by up to +14 % and −16 %. To eliminate ill-posed compensations without artificial box bounds, the objective function is regularized by a Bayesian Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) prior σproc = 0.5 % in optical thickness (OT), reflecting the physical reproducibility of the magnetron sputtering plant.

What a reflectance spectrum determines robustly is the total optical thickness: it stays within ±0.05 % of nominal across all initializations. How the total is shared between layers is what the process prior supplies, yielding well-defined, unclipped covariance uncertainties on every layer.

The photometric uncertainty is anchored in laboratory repeatability

The photometric weighting scale σ = 0.0020 (0.20 %) is anchored directly in the spectrophotometric uncertainty budget established for this instrument in the companion paper (Optics Continuum, 2026), matching the operational noise floor across the UV–MWIR for both transmittance and reflectance measurements.

Two of the three aperture parameters are not determined by these data

Between 2530 and 4000 nm both 45° coatings are spectrally flat, the cone average barely moves, and the fitted aperture walks to a bound with an uncertainty of one to two degrees. Only the first band is measured: 2.38 ± 0.08°. The run report says so; the other two numbers are not measurements.

Decoupling in situ: continuous indices are identifiable, thicknesses require the prior

In an unregularized quasi-blind test (Section 6, python tools/insitu_multistart_noprior.py), jointly inverting the three multilayer components alone across 0° and 45° (s and p) without reference single layers and without any thickness process prior (σproc → ∞) autonomously recovers the physical continuous oxide refractive indices within the single-layer uncertainty corridor (converged RMSE = 0.410%). However, layer thicknesses float into anti-correlated adjacent compensations (4.23 % RMS error, up to 12.56 % peak). This demonstrates that while reference single layers are optional for determining n(λ), an explicit process prior remains strictly necessary to pin down individual layer thicknesses.

Conventions of the optical model

Layer orderlayers[0] is adjacent to the substrate — the first layer deposited
Refractive indexN = n − ik, k ≥ 0 inside the model; tables are stored as n + ik and conjugated once, on entry
Phaseδ = 2π N d cos θ ⁄ λ
Tilted admittanceη = N cos θ (s), η = N ⁄ cos θ (p)
Incoherent substrate plateR = R_f + T_f T_f′ R_b τ² ⁄ (1 − R_f′ R_b τ²) — exact, not R_f + R_b
Polarizer modelIdeal throughout investigation (α = β = 0; crosstalk excluded); code supports Rs_meas = (1−α)Rs + αRp, Rp_meas = (1−β)Rp + βRs

They are asserted in the test suite, not only written here. A symmetric leakage leaves the half-sum invariant to 2·10⁻¹⁶; a two-side-coated plate conserves energy to 10⁻¹⁵; thicknesses perturbed by one percent come back from the spectrum they produce to 10⁻¹³ nm.

Provenance of the data

The spectra are not generic examples. They characterize coatings produced at Institut Fresnel on a Bühler Leybold Optics HELIOS 800 by plasma-assisted reactive magnetron sputtering, in March 2026, and measured on an EssentOptics PHOTON RT. The reference single-layer samples were deposited less than one week before the components, with no target change or chamber reconditioning in between — the condition that makes the transferability test legitimate.

Every measurement carries the settings the instrument recorded for it: acquisition identifier, date, stage and detector angles, polarizer, range, sampling pitch, averaging, slit width, spot size. A measurement without its conditions is not a measurement — and here those settings decided a real question, since a second acquisition of the antireflection coating turned out to have been taken with a different slit and spot, hence a different beam geometry.

The rear face is the most expensive thing to get wrong. All three experimental filters (Filter 1 AR6, Filter 2 BS45, Filter 3 BS17) sit on silicon substrates whose rear face is ground (dépolie), which scatters the return of the second interface out of the collected beam and justifies a semi-infinite model (rear = "none"). The reference single-layer samples sit on double-side polished sapphire (rear = "bare"). Mistakenly modelling a ground-rear filter as a bare polished plate adds ~30 % of parasitic Fresnel reflectance and corrupts the analysis. Two-side-coated components (rear = "coated") are also fully supported by the code and verified in the test suite.
