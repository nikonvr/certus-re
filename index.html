certus_re

Reverse engineering of multilayer optical coatings, with the degrees of freedom on the table.

Open-source implementation, measured spectra and nominal designs accompanying “Transferability of broadband UV–MWIR optical constants to the reverse engineering of sputtered multilayer filters on silicon”, F. Lemarchand and J. Lumeau (2026).

This is the second deposit of a two-part study. The first, Certus Index Spline, determined the complex refractive index of two sputtered oxides on single layers. This one asks whether that determination transfers, unchanged, to the multilayer coatings produced on the same machine — and it is built so that the question can be answered rather than assumed.

This page is a guide. README.md is the reference, and python reproduce.py is the only thing that settles an argument: it re-derives every number quoted here, in a few minutes, from two dependencies and no compiler.

Why it exists

Multilayer reverse engineering conventionally adjusts layer thicknesses and dispersion corrections inside the same optimization. When the residual falls, the improvement cannot be attributed to the thickness correction — the physically meaningful outcome — rather than to the index correction, which silently rewrites the quantity one claims to validate.

A reverse-engineering procedure that is free to reshape n(λ) cannot, by construction, establish that a previously determined n(λ) was correct.

So this package does not let a run move anything it has not declared. The parameter blocks a run may release are written in the study file, counted before the run starts by the same code that assembles the parameter vector, and printed next to every residual. A held block still appears, with the reason it is zero: 0 (imposed) and 0 (not used) are different statements, and the difference is the whole argument.

What it handles

One component or several at once, each coated on one face or on both. Every cell of that matrix is exercised by a deposited study, and tests/test_capabilities.py fails if one stops being covered.

One face coatedBoth faces coated
One component 6 layers, and 16 layers in s and p 22 unknowns on one specimen, 16 on the entrance face and 6 on the exit one
Several components two witnesses, one thickness each five specimens, two coating runs, 24 unknowns — the assembled component adds 502 measured points and none

Also: any angle of incidence, in s, p or the unpolarized channel; reflectance, transmittance and transmittance relative to the bare substrate; absorbing layers and substrates; a finite beam cone modelled as a piecewise-constant function of wavelength; polarizer leakage applied to the computed spectra; and the uncertainty the data imply on every retrieved quarter wave.

The studies are a ladder

Each rung adds exactly one thing to the one before it, so that every change in a residual can be attributed to one cause.

Rung 0 is not a fit. It releases nothing, so its residual cannot have been bought, and every other rung is read against it.

studies/volet2/counter_experiments/ holds seven studies that are wrong by construction — the design written in the wrong order, a ground rear face modelled as polished, a dispersionless substrate table, the angle shifted by a quarter of a degree, the spectral mesh decimated, the search window opened, an index correction released. They exist so that the cost of each mistake is a number a reader can reproduce rather than a warning in a paper. Each costs more than the effect the article sets out to measure.

Running it

pip install -e .
python reproduce.py                                          # the whole self-check

python -m certus_re check studies/volet2/08_joint_campaign.json   # validate, do not invert

python tools/article_tables.py        # replay the ladder, write results/
python tools/search_window_scan.py    # how the answer depends on the prior

from certus_re import load_study, count_free_parameters

Three things to know before quoting a number

The problem is ill-posed, and the deposit measures it

Many thickness vectors reproduce one spectrum to within the photometry. Widening the search window on the thicknesses from ±2 % to ±50 % changes the residual of the joint inversion by a third of the measured photometric repeatability — nothing — while the layer-to-layer dispersion of the retrieved sixteen-layer coating changes by a factor of four and a half, into a compensating pair of adjacent layers at +15 and −17 % that no coating made under optical monitoring could be.

What a reflectance spectrum determines robustly is the total optical thickness: it stays within ±0.05 % of nominal over that whole range. How the total is shared between sixteen layers is what the prior has to supply. So the search window is declared in the study file, printed beside the parameter count, and a layer that ends on it is reported.

The photometric uncertainty is measured, not specified

The campaign contains two acquisitions of one specimen four minutes apart with nothing changed. Their difference gives σ = 0.0014 over the inversion window, where the manufacturer's specification is 0.0053. With the specification the antireflection coating appears to be reproduced at the noise floor and there is nothing to explain; with the measured value the same reconstruction sits at four times the repeatability, and there is. The conclusion reversed, and neither the data nor the model had changed.

Two of the three aperture parameters are not determined by these data

Between 2530 and 4000 nm both 45° coatings are spectrally flat, the cone average barely moves, and the fitted aperture walks to a bound with an uncertainty of one to two degrees. Only the first band is measured: 2.38 ± 0.08°. The run report says so; the other two numbers are not measurements.

Conventions of the optical model

Layer orderlayers[0] is adjacent to the substrate — the first layer deposited
Refractive indexN = n − ik, k ≥ 0 inside the model; tables are stored as n + ik and conjugated once, on entry
Phaseδ = 2π N d cos θ ⁄ λ
Tilted admittanceη = N cos θ (s), η = N ⁄ cos θ (p)
Incoherent cavityR = R_f + T_f T_f′ R_b τ² ⁄ (1 − R_f′ R_b τ²) — exact, not R_f + R_b
Polarizer leakageRs_meas = (1−α)Rs + αRp, Rp_meas = (1−β)Rp + βRs, applied to the computed spectra

They are asserted in the test suite, not only written here. A symmetric leakage leaves the half-sum invariant to 2·10⁻¹⁶; a two-side-coated plate conserves energy to 10⁻¹⁵; thicknesses perturbed by one percent come back from the spectrum they produce to 10⁻¹³ nm.

Provenance of the data

The spectra are not generic examples. They characterize coatings produced at Institut Fresnel on a Bühler Leybold Optics HELIOS 800 by plasma-assisted reactive magnetron sputtering, in March 2026, and measured on an EssentOptics PHOTON RT. The single-layer witnesses were deposited less than one week before the components, with no target change or chamber reconditioning in between — the condition that makes the transferability test legitimate.

Every measurement carries the settings the instrument recorded for it: acquisition identifier, date, stage and detector angles, polarizer, range, sampling pitch, averaging, slit width, spot size. A measurement without its conditions is not a measurement — and here those settings decided a real question, since a second acquisition of the antireflection coating turned out to have been taken with a different slit and spot, hence a different beam geometry.

The rear face is the most expensive thing to get wrong. The two coatings measured alone sit on substrates whose rear face is ground, which scatters the return of the second interface out of the collected beam and is what justifies a semi-infinite model. The assembled component is a polished plate coated on both faces. Modelling the first as the second takes the computed reflectance of the antireflection coating from 14 % to 39 %.
